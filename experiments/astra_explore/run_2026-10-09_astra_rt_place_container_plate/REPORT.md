## 4. Failure analysis

Closure geometry (**[E]**, wrist position relative to the bowl centre at the first closing decision of each grasp attempt;
fingers open along x):

| attempt / decision | wrist − bowl dx (cm) | dy (cm) | closing mode | lift test | finger separation after lift (cm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 D03 | +1.0 | +2.7 | close in place (after the open descent had tilted/pushed the bowl 0.8 cm) | **held** | 5.49 |
| A2 D02 | −0.2 | +3.8 | close while lowering (replay of A1's pose) | failed | 4.90 |
| A2 D06 | +3.3 | +1.8 | close while lowering, deeper | **held** | 5.43 |
| A3 D02 | +0.9 | +2.1 | close while lowering (replay of A2's pose) | failed | 4.90 |
| A3 D06 | +1.2 | +1.4 | descend open, close in place | failed | 4.90 |
| A3 D10-D11 | +5.8 | +2.8 | off-centre rim pinch, two closing motions | **held** | 5.42 |

1. **Contact-rich grasp of a round, thin-walled object.** **[E]** All three holds were off-centre pinches of the bowl wall
   (dx 1-6 cm from the centre, separation 5.4-5.5 cm); the three centred closures (|dx| ≤ 1.2 cm) slid off and ended empty
   (4.9 cm). **[I]** With the fingers opening to 13.9 cm around an ~11 cm bowl, a centred close squeezes the rim from outside and
   the fingers slip; a pinch across one wall is what works. Astra reached this rule only in attempt 3, after two centred failures,
   and stated it as a hypothesis before testing it.
2. **Coordinate replay of a contact outcome.** **[A/E]** Attempts 2 and 3 began by replaying the previous closure pose; both
   replays failed. The remembered coordinates were of the wrist, while the outcome had depended on how the open descent had
   displaced the bowl (0.1-0.8 cm, different each time) and on the pinch geometry. The same replay strategy worked for the
   release pose, whose outcome depends only on free-space position. **[I]** Memory of where the hand was is transferable; memory
   of what the contact did is not, and the harness gave Astra no measurement of the object to make the difference visible.
3. **Perception.** Initial position hypotheses were 3-5 cm off (bowl: A1 D00; plate: A1 D05) and corrected from the head image
   within two motions; reading of contact from images was hedged and mostly confirmed by measurements. No depth-ambiguity
   failures of the LIBERO kind occurred: with absolute targets, a 5 cm error is corrected in one motion instead of accumulating.
4. **Action semantics / harness documentation.** The gripper is driven gradually over the arm's path, so a hold motion (31 steps)
   closes the fingers only partially (13.8 → 11.2 cm, then 7.4 cm on a second hold). The prompt's "~9 cm when fully closed on
   nothing" was wrong (4.9 cm); Astra detected the inconsistency and relied on lift tests. No planner failures and no rejected
   targets occurred.
5. **Insufficient feedback.** As in LIBERO, there is no force sensing; the finger separation was the only grasp signal and it is
   ambiguous during closing, so each grasp cost a lift test (one decision each: 6 of 35 decisions were tests).
6. **Infrastructure.** None. 35/35 valid structured responses; the planner-assisted interface was disclosed in the prompt and here.
7. **Budget.** Not binding: 35 of 120 calls, RoboTwin's 400-motion limit untouched.

## 5. Final assessment

What this pilot shows **[E]**: given an interface in which it names where the hand should go and the robot's own planner gets it
there, Astra completed the RoboTwin pick-and-place in all three attempts with 8-16 decisions each (about 2-5 minutes of model time
per attempt), with no policy, planner of its own, or object-pose sensor. Its procedure was the same as in LIBERO: approach from
above, correct from the image, close, verify by lifting, carry, align, lower, release, with a stated hypothesis for every motion
and all 6 grasp verdicts correct. Memory helped where the remembered quantity was a free-space pose (the release pose was reused
twice with 1 cm placement accuracy) and hurt where it was a contact outcome (both replays of a previous grasp pose failed), and
Astra's response to the second kind of failure was a genuine, evidence-based strategy change (centred close → wall pinch).

What it does not show: the number of decisions grew (8 → 11 → 16), so there is no evidence of improvement across attempts, and
with one scene and no control condition nothing causal can be said about memory. The 3/3 result depends on RoboTwin's planner
doing the motion generation; it is not comparable to the project's learned policies, which act from pixels at 14 joint positions
per step and score 48% on this task. Three attempts on one seed are not an estimate of a success rate. The rationales are Astra's
statements, not evidence of its internal process.
