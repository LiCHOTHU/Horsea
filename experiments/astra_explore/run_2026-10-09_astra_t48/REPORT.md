How to read the tables (one row per decision, generated from the logs; `REPORT_TABLES.md` has the same rows with image links
and the verbatim memory fields): "What Astra observed" is the measured state at decision time (images in
`attempt_k/decision_jj/pre_*.png`); "assessment" / "intent / hypothesis / expects" are Astra's own words **[A]**; "Executed action"

displacement in m, finger width before→after in cm, environment completion flag); "What changed in memory" diffs Astra's running
memory against the previous decision.

### 2.1 Attempt 1: success in 29 decisions (empty memory at start)

**[E] Scene at start:** ketchup upright at (−0.195, −0.130), basket centre at (0.007, 0.262), hand at (−0.048, −0.004, 0.704) with
fingers half open (4.2 cm). Note (artifact): the init file places the bottle ~3 cm into the table; the physics ejects it upward
(peak +17 cm at step 4) and it lands upright at the same (x, y), 3 cm higher than the file value, by step 8. This happens
identically in every attempt and before the hand is anywhere near the bottle; the bottle then does not move until step 90.

**Phase A, decisions 0–11 (steps 0–101): five empty closures on the upright bottle.** **[A]** D00 states the hypothesis that the
bottle is "behind and to the left" (−x, −y) and moves there while opening the fingers. **[E]** The −y part was right; the −x part
was wrong: the bottle is at x = −0.195 and the hand went to x = −0.136, still 6 cm in front of it (toward the camera). D01 descends
7 cm; at D02 Astra reports "the open fingertips alongside the ketchup neck" and closes. **[E]** At that moment the hand was 3.6 cm
in front of the bottle (x) and 5.4 cm above its centre; the fingers closed to 0.84 cm and the bottle did not move (its position is
constant to the millimetre through step 90). **[A]** D03 reads the 0.84 cm as "wider than the empty closed width ... may be a narrow
neck grasp; a lift will test whether it holds" and lifts; D04: "the hand rose 7.5 cm, but the ketchup stayed on the table and the
fingers closed to 0.2 cm. The grasp failed." It reopens and corrects +x by 2.6 cm (D04), then repeats the cycle three more times
(closures D06, D09; a deeper closure at D09 at 2.4 cm below the bottle centre), each time concluding failure from the lift test.
**[E]** The hand-to-bottle x offsets at the closures D02/D06/D09 were +3.6/+6.1/+5.1 cm; none of the closures touched the bottle.
**[I]** The corrections were in the right axis but of the wrong sign (D04 moved +x, away from the bottle) or too small, because
neither camera showed the error: the front camera looks along −x and projects a hand that is higher and nearer the camera onto the
same pixels as the bottle; the wrist camera at grasp height no longer contains the bottle (see §4).

**Phase B, decisions 12–14 (steps 101–127): the bottle is knocked over.** **[A]** D11 moves −x by 4 cm ("the hand was slightly
behind the bottle" is the stated reason, i.e. still the wrong model), D12 descends and **[E]** the fingers hit the bottle, which tips
onto its side (bottle centre z drops to 0.454 at step ~110). **[A]** D13: "the ketchup tipped onto its side instead of remaining
upright. It is now visible between the open fingers"; closure to 2.56 cm, lift test, "the grasp failed" (D15).

**Phase C, decisions 15–22 (steps 127–197): the successful body grasp.** Two more misses on the lying bottle (D17 closure
13.8 cm in front of the body, D18 lift fails). **[A]** D19 then reasons from the images: "The ketchup is now centered laterally in
the wrist view but remains below the fingertips", repositions −x by 5.5 cm (D19) and lowers (D20); D21 closes: "The bottle now
appears between the fingers in both views, supporting a closure test." **[E]** Finger width stopped at 5.67 cm (the bottle's body
diameter) and the following lift raised the bottle 4.9 cm with the hand: the first real grasp, at step 185.

**Phase D, decisions 23–28 (steps 197–265): transport and release.** **[A]** D23 moves toward +y ("carry the ketchup toward the
basket while gaining clearance"), D24 continues (+24 cm y), D25–D27 make three alignment corrections using the wrist view

basket". **[E]** The environment registered `in(ketchup, basket)` during the release, at step 265 (interaction id 29). Astra's
final running memory recorded the grasp width (5.67 cm) and the release position as facts.

   by raising the hand (A3 D28). The accidental push in A2 D02 is also what made A2's grasp possible.
4. **Insufficient feedback.** No force/contact sensing; finger width was the only grasp signal and it is ambiguous (0.84 cm empty vs
   1.09 cm narrow grasp). Astra compensated with lift tests, which cost ~8 steps and one decision each: 13 lifts ≈ 110 steps, about
   37% of an attempt's horizon.
5. **Repeated mistakes.** **[E]** A3's five misses on the upright bottle with 2–3 cm alternating corrections against a 6–11 cm error;
   A1 D04's correction had the wrong sign (+x, away from the bottle) because the stated model ("the hand was slightly behind the
   bottle") was inverted. **[I]** The corrections were driven by qualitative image reading, not by a measurement of the error.
6. **Unsupported assumptions.** **[A]** "The open fingers visibly straddle the ketchup's upper section" (A3 D02, D06, D09, D13, D16) is
   not supported by the saved images; the attempt-3 premise that attempt 2's hand coordinates would reproduce the grasp ignored that
   attempt 2's bottle had first been pushed 3.3 cm toward the hand. **[E]** Astra's memory records the attempt-2 closure position but
   not the collision that preceded it (its D03 assessment mentions the drift, its later summaries do not).
7. **Infrastructure errors.** None in the live run (0 CLI errors, 0 timeouts, 0 retries, 0 isolation violations, 78/78 valid
   structured responses). The first launch was aborted before any model call by a sandbox permission error (Codex could not read
   `~/.codex/rules`); fixed with `--ignore-rules` and verified offline before relaunch (§1).
8. **Budget exhaustion.** None. Attempt 3 ended by Astra's stop request with 4 steps and 1 model call remaining; the stop is recorded
   as `model_stopped`, distinct from success and from budget exhaustion.
9. **Where the evidence cannot identify the cause.** Whether the successful narrow grasp in attempt 2 was on the cap, the neck or a
   tilted shoulder cannot be determined from the logs (object orientation was not recorded, and the wrist image shows no bottle at
   closure time). Whether Astra's alignment claims reflect misreading the images or filling in from expectation cannot be decided
   from stated rationales.

## 5. Final assessment

What this pilot shows **[E]**: with no policy, planner, or object-pose access, Astra completed the pick-and-place in 2 of 3 attempts
on one fixed scene, entirely through native end-effector delta commands chosen one decision at a time. Its behaviour had a
recognisable experimental structure: it stated a hypothesis for almost every action (77 of 78), predicted the visible change,
verified every grasp with a lift before transporting, read the lift's outcome correctly every time, and rewrote a bounded memory
with cited interaction ids. The memory mattered where the relevant quantity was measurable: attempt 2 reused attempt 1's release
coordinates and completed the transport in 2 moves instead of 5. Its honesty was also measurable: it never claimed success, and it
ended attempt 3 with an explicit "stop without claiming success" when the horizon made recovery impossible.

What it also shows: the binding limitation was perception of depth, not reasoning. Twelve of fifteen closures were several
centimetres in front of the bottle, Astra's image-based alignment statements were wrong at those moments, and its corrections were
small and sometimes wrong in sign because no observation quantified the error. The one fast success depended on an accidental
collision; trying to *replay* that success by coordinates (attempt 3) failed because the collision was not part of the remembered
cause. Attempt 3 therefore ended worse than attempt 1 despite more memory: performance across attempts was 29 → 10 → ∞ decisions,
not monotone.

What this pilot does not establish: it cannot show that memory *causes* faster success (one scene, no memory-ablated control, and
attempt 2's speed is partly luck); it cannot show sample efficiency relative to any policy or baseline; it cannot show
generalisation to other scenes, objects or tasks (one init state, one task); and the rationales are Astra's stated reasons, not
evidence of its internal process. The run also used 1.5 s of model time per simulated 0.03 s of robot time (median 17.8 s per
decision), which bounds any claim about practical closed-loop control.
