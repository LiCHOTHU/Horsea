# Looped and randomized transformers: what the cited papers show, and what it means for Horsea

Sources: the Zhihu answer by MoonCancer, and the 7 papers it cites. Each paper was read in full on 2026-09-24.

## Looping

| Paper | What is looped | Key evidence | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Looped Transformers for Length Generalization (2409.15647, ICLR'25) | One GPT-2 block. Input embeddings are re-injected every loop. The block is supervised only at the ground-truth step count T(n). | Trained up to length 20. Parity is ≈1.0 at length 40; addition ≈1.0 at 30; multiplication ≈0.92 at 16. Baselines score about 0 to 0.5. | Needs the oracle T(n). A fixed loop count fails (addition 0.07). Per-sample halting works only when the loop reaches a fixed point; parity stays at chance. Toy tasks only. |
| Reasoning with Latent Thoughts (2502.17416, ICLR'25) | A k-layer block looped L times, with no input injection. | 1⊗12 ≈ 12⊗1 on addition and p-hop. At 1B LM scale, looping costs perplexity (7.40 → 7.90 for 12⊗2) but matches or beats the baseline on math and reasoning primitives: math 34.3 vs 29.3, primitives 51–57 vs 47.5. A cosine regularizer that makes layers similar gives PPL 7.38 and math 36.4 at λ=10, but hurts at λ=1. | Single seeds. The gains concentrate in pointer-like retrieval. No FLOP savings. Theory covers expressivity only. |
| Recurrent Depth / Huginn (2502.05171) | Prelude → a shared 4-layer core iterated r times from a random s0, with input injection by concatenation every step → coda. r ~ log-normal Poisson (mean 32). Truncated backprop over 8 steps. 3.5B params, 800B tokens. | Accuracy grows with r (GSM8K keeps improving to r=32–64; HellaSwag saturates by r≈8). GSM8K 42 vs 29 for OLMo-7B. A per-token KL early exit works without training (MMLU: 12.7–16.2 steps). Latent states form fixed points, orbits and sliders. | Useless at r=1. Two collapsed runs at scale (tokens collapse; model ignores its recurrent state). Conditioning the core on the step index destroyed extrapolation. Adding noise per step did not help. Gains are not FLOP-matched. |
| CoT Solves Inherently Serial Problems (2402.12875, ICLR'24) | Theory: T CoT steps ≈ a size-T circuit. Without CoT, constant precision gives AC0 and log precision gives TC0. | S5 composition, CVP and iterated squaring: 100% with CoT vs chance without. | The Zhihu claim "transformers can't do parity or majority" is overstated. It holds only at constant precision; with log precision, parity is in TC0. Separations are conditional. |

## Randomization

| Paper | Evidence | Caveats |
|---|---|---|
| Learning Randomized Algorithms with Transformers (2408.10818) | Random seed input plus a worst-case (q-norm) loss over m seeds per input. The model learns a mixed strategy: it beats the deterministic model on worst-case inputs, and majority voting over seeds approaches 100% on associative recall and 3-colouring. | Plain ERM zeros out the seed (Yao's principle): randomness never helps the average case. Toy scale; m× compute. |
| Mahoney, Randomized Linear Algebra notes (1608.04481) | Sampled matmul costs O(mnc), with error ‖A‖_F‖B‖_F/√c and p ∝ ‖A_col‖‖B_row‖. | The Zhihu claim "mnk → mn log k" is wrong. c is set by the target accuracy (≈1/ε²), not by log k. |
| Skeinformer (2112.05359) | Sketched attention with pilot sampling and row normalization: LRA average 58.1 vs 57.5 for standard attention. | A "V-mean" baseline that ignores attention beats both on Text and Retrieval, so the "regularization" gain is mostly an LRA artifact. |

## What this means for our FM policy and memory study
1. **Our flow sampler is already a looped model, but a different kind.** Huginn iterates toward a fixed point that doesn't depend on its random start. An FM sampler integrates a *time-conditioned* field, and the noise *chooses* the action mode. Huginn's step-conditioning ablation and noise ablation both failed, so the analogy is loose. More Euler steps reduce discretization error; they do not add reasoning. Cheap check: success vs number of denoising steps (1, 2, 4, 8, 16) on the base.
2. **Input injection is the transferable result.** Every looped model works only when the conditioning is re-injected at every iteration (Huginn's Bad Run 2 learned to ignore its state; length-gen's ablation dropped without injection). This supports reading the memory at every velocity evaluation (KV, FM-write, TTT arms), not once (residual arm) and not only through the initial noise. The study already tests this contrast.
3. **Where a loop could actually help:** loop the DiT blocks *inside* each velocity call (middle-looping was the most balanced in Saunshi et al.), trained with random depth. That multiplies the cost of each call, and it only pays off if LIBERO failures are compositional (long-horizon / state tracking) rather than visuomotor precision. Worth diagnosing from failure cases first.
4. **Writing memory during denoising is risky; writing between chunks is supported.** Huginn's step-conditioned-operator failure suggests freezing the fast weights during the ODE and writing between decisions, which is the current design.
5. **Randomization:** an FM policy is already a randomized algorithm (noise = seed), but ERM training doesn't turn the noise into a hedge. A per-init-state success histogram across seeds would tell us whether resampling or best-of-N could help. Worst-case (q-norm over init states) RL fine-tuning is the paper-faithful test. Randomized matmul/sketching brings no compute benefit at 16 tokens; its one useful idea is the ‖δ‖‖x‖ importance score for selecting memory writes.
