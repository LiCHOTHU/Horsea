Per decision and per hand: an absolute wrist target in the world frame (position plus two directions: where the straightened fingers point and where the palm faces). The harness converts this to the IK pose and tracks it with the benchmark's own differential-IK controllers, the same configuration as the project's evaluation agent: left DLS, right pseudo-inverse. The commanded pose moves along a straight line at <= 1 cm and 3° per 30 Hz step. Each hand gets four closure fractions in [0, 1] mapped onto its 12 joints: `finger_base` (4 knuckles), `finger_tip` (4 middle joints), `thumb_rotate`, `thumb_bend` (3 thumb joints); these change by <= 1/30 per step. ## 2. Step-by-step account of every attempt

### 2.1 Attempt 1: stopped by Astra after 15 decisions (374 of 400 steps)

**Finding a reachable hand orientation (D00-D03).** **[A]** D00 plans "a downward grasp": fingers straight down, palm facing
forward, wrist above the can. **[E]** The wrist ends 27.3 cm from target with 153° orientation error; the fingers end up pointing
up. **[A]** D01: "The fingers point upward, so the intended downward approach failed"; it tries "a horizontal, palm-down
orientation". **[E]** 16.9 cm / 142°. **[A]** D02 hypothesises that "an inward-facing palm may avoid the wrist joint limits
encountered with the previous orientations" (fingers forward, palm facing the robot's left). **[E]** 4.0 cm / 25.5°; at D03,
lowering beside the can, 0.73 cm / 2.1°. **[A]** "The inward-facing pose tracked much better." **[I]** In three decisions it
found the same reachable orientation the author's probes found (palm facing left, fingers forward), reasoning only from the
reported residuals.

**Closing beside the can (D04-D10).** **[E]** Each approach displaced the can. It shifted left and tilted at D03, and at D05-D08 it
was pushed 9 cm to the left and tipped against the hand. **[A]** Astra reads this from the images ("The can shifted left and
tilted instead of remaining upright, suggesting incidental contact"). It closes three times (D04, D06, D08), each followed by
the same correct reading of the measurements: a thumb-index gap of 1.8-2.2 cm (the empty-hand value) with zero index contact
means the closure "likely missed". **[A]** D09 runs an explicit retention test: "Lift the closed right hand 12 cm to test can
retention". **[E]** The wrist rose 12.6 cm; the can stayed on the table. **[A]** D10: "The lift confirms that the previous
closure did not retain it."

**Hitting the cabinet and stopping (D11-D14).** **[A]** D12 retries the downward-finger approach ("a higher wrist with downward
fingers can reach around the can"). **[E]** 17.3 cm / 103° error, and the index finger presses on the cabinet at 554 N. **[A]**
D13: "The fingers visibly press against the wooden panel." D13 moves the hand right to clear the panel (**[E]** contact back to
0 N, error 24.8 cm / 142°). **[A]** D14 stops with 26 steps left: "End this unsuccessful attempt so a fresh attempt can use a
different grasp approach." Its final memory says "A fresh attempt should establish a reachable grasp orientation in free space
and confirm can alignment before closure."

### 2.2 Attempt 2: horizon reached after 23 decisions

**Carried over.** **[A]** D00's hypothesis cites attempt 1: "Forward-pointing fingers with the palm down may track better than
the downward-finger orientation that failed previously" (references 10-14, all from attempt 1). **[E]** Palm-down tracked only
partly: 6.2 cm / 34° after 40 steps. **[A]** D01 notices from the reported path length: "Only 40 of 51 commanded path steps
elapsed, so the pose has not yet had time to finish". It repeats the same target for 18 more steps (**[E]** 3.4 cm / 24.5°).

**Top-down closures (D02-D11).** Astra closes over the can top with a partly tracked palm-down hand at D03, D06 and D09
(fractions 0.45-0.6 knuckles, 0.55-0.65 tips, 0.6-0.65 thumb rotation). Each time it lift-tests (D04, D10) and correctly
reports the miss: "Full closure, a 2.1 cm gap, and zero index contact support a missed grasp". **[A]** It also uses a new
signal: at D04, "Knuckle closure stopped at 0.34 versus 0.6 commanded, which could indicate contact elsewhere on the hand",
which it later revises as "Earlier knuckle obstruction may have been table contact" (final hypothesis).

**Adopting the achieved orientation (D12).** **[A]** After a 9.3 cm tracking miss, D12 commands the orientation the hand had
actually reached (fingers (0.87, 0.44, -0.23), palm (-0.32, 0.15, -0.94)): "Keeping the achieved orientation may improve pose
tracking." **[E]** From then on tracking errors are 0.05-0.7 cm and 0.0-1.2° (D12-D22, except D16's 2.5 cm stop). **[I]** This is
the most transferable lesson of the run: it turned the IK's own behaviour into a usable orientation.

**Precise but empty closures (D13-D22).** **[E]** Three more closures (D13, D18, D21), each lift-tested (D14, D19, D22). All
closed to 1.5-2.3 cm with zero index contact, and the can never rose more than 3 cm. **[A]** The assessments track this
precisely, e.g. D20: "The wrist rose 10.66 cm, but the can stayed on the table ... The lift confirms failed retention". D22 spends
the last 6 steps on a 5 cm retention test. Final memory: "Accurate wrist tracking alone has not established can alignment. A
future attempt needs clearer can-to-finger localization before repeated closure."

### 2.3 Attempt 3: horizon reached after 27 decisions; the can was lifted 14.5 cm, then lost

**Carried over.** **[A]** D00: "Position the open right hand above the can using an orientation that previously tracked well".
**[E]** It reuses attempt 2's late orientation and reaches the raised pose within 0.16 cm / 0.4°.

**Search for the closing region (D01-D11).** Closures at D04 and D08 (lift tests D05, D09) miss again. **[E]** The approach and
closure at D02-D05 tip the can by up to ~55° and push it 4 cm forward (toward the cabinet); it settles upright again. **[A]** Two explicit geometric corrections follow. D03: "The can is behind and
on the thumb side of the current closing region; withdrawing the wrist should improve alignment". D11: "Earlier grasps closed
behind the can; a modest forward correction during descent will place its upper body within the closing region", and it notes
that "closure will retract [the fingers] toward the wrist".

**The lift (D12-D14).** D12 closes with lower thumb bending than before (fractions 0.60 / 0.65 / 0.55 / 0.30), wrist at (0.30,
-0.15, 1.15). **[E]** The index contact force reads 13.7 N, the first non-zero contact on the can in the run, and the knuckles
stop at 0.37 against 0.60 commanded. **[A]** D13: "this is stronger evidence of capture than previous grasps, but retention
remains unverified"; it lift-tests 15 cm. **[E]** The wrist rises 14.8 cm and the can rises from z 1.021 to 1.166 (+14.5 cm) with
11.1 N contact. **[A]** D14: "The wrist rose 14.83 cm and the can visibly rose with it. Persistent 11.05 N index contact and
obstructed knuckle closure support successful retention. The can bottom is still close to the compartment lip." It plans the
next step toward the saucer: "Additional vertical clearance will let the can cross the front lip without collision" (7 cm in 10
steps). **[E]** The can slips out during this lift and falls back to the table (evaluation log after D14: (0.326, -0.100), z 1.049,
tilted ~29°; after D15 still leaning ~28°). **[A]** D15: "The additional
lift lost the can: it is now on the tabletop, index contact fell to zero". **[I]** The grip at D13 was marginal: contact only at
the index link, knuckles blocked at 0.37. A second fast lift (0.7 cm per step, the interface maximum) was enough to shake the can
out. A slower lift, or adding knuckle / thumb closure before moving, would have tested the hypothesis without risking the can.
Astra did neither.

**Recovery (D15-D26).** Four more closures (D18, D21, D23, D26), one with the fingers tilted 45° down toward the fallen can (D17-D19; 0.71 cm / 11° error on the way, 30° after the lift). All lift tests are empty (2.2-2.8 cm gap, zero
contact). D26 spends the last 8 steps on one more closure. Final memory: "An earlier captured can slipped during lifting
(52,53) ... Repeated closures and lift tests have failed to establish reliable retention."

**Did Astra use its memory across attempts?** Yes, in two concrete ways. **[E]** Attempt 2's first decision cites
attempt 1's interactions 10-14 and avoids the downward-finger pose that had failed there. Attempt 3's first decision reuses
the orientation attempt 2 had converged on ("an orientation that previously tracked well") and tracks within 0.16 cm and 0.4°
on its first motion. The time spent finding a usable pose fell from 4 decisions (attempt 1) and 13 (attempt 2) to 1
(attempt 3). The lift-test protocol introduced in attempt 1 (close, then lift and see whether the can comes along) was kept in
every attempt: 12 lift tests in total, each read correctly.

**Which hypotheses were tested, and how did they end?** [A]-[E] pairs: "an inward-facing palm may avoid the wrist joint
limits" (confirmed: 153° → 2° error); "forward-pointing fingers with the palm down may track better" (partly: 34° → 17°);
"keeping the achieved orientation may improve pose tracking" (confirmed: < 1.2° for the rest of attempt 2); "earlier grasps
closed behind the can; a modest forward correction ... will place its upper body within the closing region" (followed by the
only grasp that held); "additional vertical clearance will let the can cross the front lip" (the can was lost during the
lift that tested it).

**Did it repeat failed actions?** Partly. The same family of closures, right hand only and palm-down or palm-left, was
repeated 15 times with 1-5 cm wrist corrections in between. Each was measured and verified, but the grasp type never changed.
No decision moved the can to a better position first, used the left hand, or slowed a lift after a marginal grasp.

**Bimanual use.** None. **[E]** The left arm held its start pose for all 65 decisions; the door joint stayed at 0. **[I]** The
right order (can first) kept the door subtask out of reach because the can was never placed. Astra also never tried the left
hand as a helper for the can, for example as a second palm to squeeze against, which was the author's main idea in the
feasibility check (where it fell 1.6 cm short of reach).

**How Astra treated evidence.** It never claimed success or capture without a test. It combined four signals: the thumb-index
distance (empty ≈ 2 cm), index contact force, measured versus commanded knuckle closure ("obstructed closure"), and the lift
test. It labelled the result accordingly: "capture unconfirmed", "stronger evidence of capture ... but retention remains
unverified", "the lift confirms failed retention". In attempt 2 it revised its own earlier inference ("Earlier knuckle
obstruction may have been table contact").

**Author's feasibility check versus Astra [I].** The author's ~40 privileged scripted grasps never lifted the can. Astra, without
object poses, lifted it once in 15 closures, with a grasp the author had not tried: a near-palm-down hand (palm (-0.09, 0.23,
-0.97), fingers (0.88, 0.47, 0.03)), knuckles 0.60, tips 0.65, thumb rotation 0.55 and thumb bend 0.30, over the can's upper body.
So the task is not shown to be infeasible through this interface. A solution additionally needs a secure grip, transport over
the cabinet lip to the saucer at x = 0.65, a release, withdrawal of the right arm, and the left hand's door push, all within 400
steps. That remains undemonstrated by anyone.

## 5. Final assessment

- **Success rate: 0/3.** Best progress: the can lifted 14.5 cm off the table (attempt 3, decision 13). The can never reached the
  saucer and the door was never touched.
- **Exploration quality.** High in its local loop. Every decision states an intent, a hypothesis and an expected change. The next
  decision's assessment compares that with the measured effect, and memory carries confirmed facts with interaction ids.
  Astra found the reachable orientations on its own and used the trick of adopting the achieved pose. It built a reliable
  retention test, and its success and capture claims were always backed by measurements.
- **Where it fell short.** It localized the can relative to the hand's closing region poorly, which cost 14 empty closures. It
  did not protect the one good grip (a fast second lift). It never changed strategy at the level of the task: no left hand, no
  repositioning of the can, no plan for the door. On a 400-step horizon, 15-27 small decisions per attempt were not enough to
  get past the first subtask.
- **Compared with the earlier pilots** (same model and protocol): LIBERO single-arm pick-place 2/3, RoboTwin single-arm 3/3
  (planner-assisted), RoboTwin bimanual pot lift 1/3 (planner-assisted), this Isaac dex-hand bimanual task 0/3 (IK-assisted, no
  planner). The drop tracks interface assistance and contact difficulty: with dexterous hands and no grasp planner the can
  grasp alone consumed all three attempts.
