## 4. Failure analysis

1. **Reachability (arm-specific).** **[E]** 17 planner failures, 15 of them for the right arm; all failures were for targets that were either high (≥ 1.05 m) or inside the left arm's half of the table (x ≤ 0). The prompt gave only a workspace box, not the per-arm reach, so Astra discovered the limits by failing; it learned the height limit (attempt 3 starts lower) but not the lateral one in time to save attempt 1.
2. **Contact-rich bimanual grasp.** **[E]** A symmetric close captured one handle and missed the other in attempts 1 and 2 (the pot is yawed and offset, so symmetric wrist targets put one gripper 2-3 cm off its handle). The successful grasp used asymmetric targets after a visual judgement of the offset and two settling holds. Finger separation again proved ambiguous during closing (11 cm after one hold could be either), which is why the settling holds mattered.
3. **Strategy under partial success (author's inference).** **[I]** In attempt 1, holding the pot tilted with one arm while re-grasping with the other turned a recoverable state into an unrecoverable one (the pot was pushed out of the right arm's reach). Opening both grippers and restarting the two-arm alignment, which Astra chose only at D21 after the pot had already moved, would have cost fewer decisions. In attempt 2 a lateral move with the pot hanging from one gripper lost the object entirely.
4. **Lid collision.** **[E/A]** One occurrence, diagnosed from the blocked descent and fixed by rotating the fingers; this was the best single piece of reasoning in the run.
5. **Harness.** **[E]** The orientation-reference table and the simultaneous-motion statement were used as intended (A1 D05 picked the "pointing down, fingers along y" entry). No infrastructure errors, no budget exhaustion; both stops were Astra's own, and the success came 31 calls under the total budget.

## 5. Final assessment

What this shows **[E]**: on a task that cannot be solved by one arm, Astra reached success in the third attempt with 9 decisions, after two
attempts that it ended itself. The improvement across attempts is traceable in the records to specific things it learned and kept: the lid
collision fix (fingers along y), the reachable approach height, settling holds before trusting a closure, and small coordinated lift tests.
Its honesty held: it never claimed success, correctly inferred that it had lost the pot in attempt 2, and stopped both failed attempts with
stated reasons.

What it also shows: when one of two grasps fails, Astra's instinct was to fix the failed side while holding with the other, and twice that
made things worse (a pushed-out-of-reach pot; a pot flung off the table). It did not know the arms' reach and paid 17 failed motions to learn
it. Three attempts on one scene cannot say how often the third-attempt success would recur, and the planner-assisted interface again does the
motion generation; the comparison with the project's learned policies (28% on this task from pixels) is not like for like.
